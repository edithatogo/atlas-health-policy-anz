# Credentialing and scope-of-clinical-practice collection

## Purpose

This collection provides an evidence-first catalogue of Australian credentialing and scope-of-clinical-practice authorities, implementation guidance and model scopes. It supports comparative policy analysis and local adaptation without treating a model scope or another jurisdiction's procedure as operative local authority.

## Medallion treatment

### Bronze

Bronze contains publisher identity, public source URL, observed date, rights and access notes, fixity where a binary is lawfully preserved, and an explicit source-status field. A catalogue entry is not represented as a locally preserved current binary unless those bytes and their provenance are present.

### Silver

Silver may contain normalised metadata and bounded text derived from public sources. Extracted text remains linked to the source document, page or source location, extraction method, confidence and review state.

### Gold

Gold contains authority classifications, source-to-requirement crosswalks, model-SoCP structure, comparator patterns and candidate local controls. It must distinguish binding law or directive, approved policy, guidance, model content, historical comparator and analyst inference.

### Platinum

Platinum contains decision briefs, gap analyses, implementation roadmaps and visual maps. It cannot promote an inferred rule into a mandatory requirement without a current source and accountable human review.

## Catalogue boundary

The observed NSW State Scope of Clinical Practice Unit catalogue contains 88 entries:

- 56 medical and surgical entries;
- 20 paediatric entries; and
- 12 dental entries.

Of these, 83 were listed as final and five as under review after consultation: General Practice, Neurology, Neurosurgery, Orthopaedic Surgery and Urology.

The inventory is a source-discovery and comparison asset. Health services outside NSW remain responsible for monitoring changes and for making their own facility-, service- and practitioner-specific decisions.

## CHHHS adaptation rules

A source model may be used to reduce avoidable reinvention, but CHHHS adoption requires:

1. confirmation of the current source and licence or permission position;
2. reconciliation with current national and Queensland requirements;
3. a source-to-local crosswalk;
4. profession and relevant-peer review;
5. service and facility capability assessment, including CSCF where applicable;
6. rural, remote and First Nations context;
7. approval through the CHHHS document and credentialing governance pathways;
8. versioning, transition and upstream-change surveillance; and
9. a practitioner-specific human decision where the model is applied.

Rural Generalist Medicine should be developed as a CHHHS priority using current specialty-recognition material and advanced-skill modules. General Practice and thin paediatric-network models may be comparators, but are not substitutes for a locally validated Rural Generalist model.

## Information and AI boundary

No patient, practitioner, credential, referee, committee-case, tenant or production-system material belongs in the public collection. OCR, NLP, retrieval and generative tools may assist discovery, extraction, comparison and drafting, but every material claim must retain source provenance and a human review state. AI cannot determine competence, scope, conditions, approval, appeal or release to practise.

## Publication status

The collection is metadata-first. Source binaries are published only where document-specific rights, integrity and publication approval are established. A failed download, missing binary or inaccessible source remains visible as a gap rather than being silently reconstructed.
